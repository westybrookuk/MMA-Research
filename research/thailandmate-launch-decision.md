## 1. How the scoring works

Five cities × four personas = **20 combinations**. Each is scored **1–5** on six criteria. For *every* criterion, **a higher score is better for ThailandMate at the MVP stage** (the two "cost/risk" criteria are phrased positively):

| Criterion | 5/5 means… | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **User pain** | Frequent, acute, currently-unsolved problems ThailandMate can address | 20% |
| **Search / discovery potential** | Large, active audience already looking for help (judged from community size, content volume and forum activity observed in research — **not** measured search traffic) | 20% |
| **Willingness to pay** | Strong evidence the segment spends money on solutions | 20% |
| **Affiliate / lead-gen potential** | Multiple programmatic, high-value revenue options | 20% |
| **Maintenance ease** | Content stays accurate with little upkeep (**5 = low burden**; 1 = constant rule-churn/liability) | 10% |
| **Trust / safety** | Brand-safe, low legal & reputational risk (**5 = low risk**; 1 = high risk) | 10% |

**Weighted total** = pain·0.20 + discovery·0.20 + WTP·0.20 + affiliate·0.20 + maintenance·0.10 + trust·0.10.

### How to read the scores
- **Chiang Mai × Digital nomads (4.1) is the clear wedge.** It pairs the largest, fastest-moving demand pool with the most acute *recent* pains (2025–26 DTV banking exclusion, the 180-day tax clock, annual burning-season migration) and a brand-safe, cheap-to-research city. [FACT basis: Kasikorn's published DTV exclusion; DTV 35,000+ first-year applications; IQAir AQI 263 on 29 Mar 2026 — see opportunity report.]
- **Retiree combos cluster high (3.8–4.0)** across Phuket, Chiang Mai, Pattaya and Hua Hin because WTP and affiliate/lead value (insurance, property, visa services) are strong. Hua Hin ties the group on risk-adjusted appeal (highest trust score, most under-covered in English). [REC] treat retirees as the immediate **adjacent** segment, not the first.
- **Families and workers/entrepreneurs score well on raw WTP/affiliate (esp. Bangkok 3.7–3.9) but are gated** by high maintenance burden, legal liability ([LEGAL-RISK] tax/business/school rules), long sales cycles, and — for Bangkok — the strongest incumbent presence. They are **phase-2, lead-gen-first**, not MVP content.
- **Two adjustments the numeric scores don't capture [ASSUMPTION]:**
  1. **Competitive density.** Bangkok is the stronghold of the best-resourced incumbent (Thailand Starter Kit runs its paid relocation from Bangkok); a new entrant's *effective* opportunity there is lower than the raw score.
  2. **Hua Hin discovery is understated.** Its current search/content volume is low *because it is under-served* — low competition, not low demand. This is why it earns fast-follow status despite a middling discovery score.

## 3. Recommendations

### 3.1 First audience to prioritise → **Digital nomads / remote workers on the DTV**
**[REC]** English-speaking remote workers and freelancers, roughly 25–45, planning or in their first year in Thailand.
- **Why:** largest and fastest-growing addressable pool (DTV drew 35,000+ applications in year one [FACT]); most online and distributable; their top pains are recent, recurring and poorly solved by neutral content (banking exclusion, tax-residency cliff, compliance admin, burning season).
- **Why not retirees first:** retirees have higher WTP but slower, higher-trust, higher-liability sales (insurance/legal) and skew to forums/email rather than viral distribution. Nomads give **top-of-funnel volume and feedback speed**; retirees give **monetisation depth** one step later.

### 3.2 First city / city pair → **Chiang Mai first, paired with Hua Hin**
**[REC]**
- **Chiang Mai = the wedge** (score 4.1): demand density, search volume, low cost for the team to validate, universal hook.
- **Hua Hin = the paired second city**: (a) the natural, brand-safe answer to Chiang Mai's Feb–Apr burning season; (b) the bridge to the retiree segment; (c) the most under-covered of the five in English.
- This is a **city pair**, not five parallel bets. Content cross-links: "Chiang Mai burning season → escape to Hua Hin."

### 3.3 Should all five cities appear in the public MVP? → **No — two deep, one broad comparison page**
**[REC]**
- **Deep at launch:** Chiang Mai (full guides + tools).
- **Fast-follow (weeks 5–10):** Hua Hin deep content.
- **All five appear only as one lightweight "Best city for you" comparison page** (a table/quiz covering Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Phuket, Pattaya, Hua Hin). This is cheap to build, captures broad "where to live in Thailand" search, and routes users to the two deep cities — without creating shallow, stale, trust-damaging pages for the other three.
- **Do not** publish per-city deep dives for Bangkok/Phuket/Pattaya until demand is validated. Half-maintained city pages are a trust liability in a domain where rules change yearly.

### 3.4 First paid product and a realistic test price
**[REC] Product:** a digital, self-serve **"First 90 Days in Thailand — Chiang Mai Edition"** pack: dated checklists and worksheets for visa/TDAC/TM30/90-day compliance, a **banking-by-visa-status playbook**, SIM/eSIM/connectivity setup, a neighbourhood + rental-lease checklist, a healthcare/insurance selector, and a **180-day tax-residency tracker**. Free lead magnet: the visa comparator + tax tracker.
**[REC] Test price:** presale at **US$29 vs US$49 (A/B)**, with a stated early-bird/list price of **$49**.
- Rationale for the band [ASSUMPTION]: it sits deliberately below the cheapest verified human-service touchpoint (Thailand Starter Kit's **US$100** consultation; Baan Smile's **US$149** video consult [FACT]) and occupies the empty gap between free articles and done-for-you service ($1,500/$2,600 at TSK [FACT]). The exact number is a hypothesis — the presale tests it. **Do not anchor on any unobserved willingness-to-pay.**

### 3.5 First three monetisation categories to test
**[REC]**
1. **Insurance** — nomad travel/medical (e.g. SafetyWing/Genki-type) via affiliate; retiree O-A-compliant cover (Pacific Cross/Luma-type) via **broker lead-gen**. Highest fit: every arrival needs it, retirees are *required* to hold it. **[LEGAL-RISK]** O-A insurer lists/minimums change; link OIC/longstay.tgia.org.
2. **Connectivity** — eSIM (Airalo/Nomad-type) + VPN affiliate. Low-friction, high-frequency, first-week purchase.
3. **Visa / legal lead-generation** — DTV extension, retirement O/O-A, and LTR/Privilege referrals to **vetted, licensed** partners. Highest long-term value; **[LEGAL-RISK]** never present as regulated advice; disclose commercial relationships.

(Housing/rental agent leads are the strong #4 once city content ranks; money-transfer (Wise-type) is a natural in-content link for the banking story.)

## 4. What we are deliberately NOT doing at MVP
- **[REC]** No Bangkok/Phuket/Pattaya deep content at launch (comparison page only).
- **[REC]** No family/entrepreneur flagship products — those become lead-gen to vetted schools/lawyers in phase 2.
- **[REC]** No DIY tax or legal advice — tools *track and explain*; professionals advise. Every [LEGAL-RISK] surface links the official source and carries a last-updated date + disclaimer.
- **[REC]** No subscription product until an email list exists (phase-2 "rule-change alert" membership).

## 5. Verified facts vs assumptions vs recommendations

### Verified facts [FACT] (sourced in the opportunity report; re-verify before publishing)
- DTV: 5-year multi-entry, 180 days/entry (+180 extension ~1,900 THB), 500,000 THB proof of funds, ~10,000 THB fee; remote work for overseas clients allowed without a work permit; apply from outside via thaievisa.go.th. 35,000+ applications in year one.
- Retirement (50+): 800,000 THB or 65,000 THB/month; O-A requires OIC-approved insurance (national minimum 400k/40k; some embassies up to 3m); in-country Non-O has no mandatory insurance.
- LTR (BOI): 10-year, ~US$80k income test with variants, overseas-income tax exemption for wealth/remote categories, annual reporting, digital work permit.
- Thailand Privilege: paid tiers from 650,000 THB (Bronze promo) to 5,000,000 THB (Reserve).
- Banking: major banks (Kasikorn, Bangkok Bank, SCB) require non-immigrant visas; Kasikorn's published terms explicitly exclude tourist/VOA/free-visa/**DTV**; 2025–26 tightening followed the mule-account crackdown; LTR/Privilege get fast-track onboarding.
- Tax: residents (180+ days) may be taxed on foreign income remitted to Thailand under the 2024 change; DTV has no tax provision; LTR wealth/remote categories are exempt. **[LEGAL-RISK]**
- Cost anchors: Bangkok ~28–39% more expensive than Chiang Mai (Numbeo/LivingCost); international school tuition Bangkok ~280k–1.2m THB/yr (ISB KG–G5 ~1.015m for 2026/27).
- Competitor pricing (verified): Thailand Starter Kit free guides/tools + US$100 consult / US$1,500 30-day / US$2,600 1-year (Bangkok-focused); Baan Smile US$149 video consult.

### Assumptions [ASSUMPTION] (to test — not facts)
- All 20 city/persona scores and their relative ranking.
- Nomads will pay $29–49 for a digital pack rather than use free content.
- Hua Hin has sufficient demand to support a second city (low competition vs low demand is unproven).
- Retirees are the highest-LTV adjacent segment.
- A free tool (visa comparator / tax tracker) will convert visitors to email at ~10%.
- Thai providers (agents, insurers, brokers) will pay for leads — unverified, needs outreach.

### Recommendations [REC] (decisions to make/validate)
- Audience: DTV digital nomads first; retirees adjacent.
- Geography: Chiang Mai wedge + Hua Hin pair; other three via one comparison page only.
- Product: "First 90 Days — Chiang Mai" digital pack; test $29 vs $49 presale.
- Revenue order: insurance → connectivity → visa/legal lead-gen.
- Validate in 30 days for under ~US$100 (see `thailandmate-validation-kit.md`) before building breadth.

### Open items requiring verification before any launch content
- Current affiliate program terms/rates (insurance, eSIM, VPN, transfer, accommodation) — confirm in partner portals; never publish rates.
- Any 2026 change to visa-exempt entry length (60→30 days was reported as *approved/pending* by one secondary source; **not** officially confirmed — treat as rumour until MFA/Immigration confirms).
- DTV *approval* (not application) numbers; embassy-specific document lists.
- Banking policy at each major bank, re-checked at publish time (changes quarterly).
- Provider lead-gen willingness and rate (direct outreach).
